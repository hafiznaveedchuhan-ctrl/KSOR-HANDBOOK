A listing is the compact unit a reader scans before deciding whether to
click at all — it has to do in a few seconds what a full review does in
several paragraphs. A roundup, a comparison table, or a homepage grid lives
or dies on how well its individual listings are built, independent of how
good the surrounding writing is.

## Anatomy of a High-Converting Listing

- **Image**: the product's real Amazon image, sourced properly (see
  `product-sourcing.md`) — never a placeholder or an unrelated stock photo.
- **Title**: the actual product name, not a SKU or an abbreviated internal
  label.
- **Price**: shown with a "price may vary" note. Amazon does not allow a
  static price to be portrayed as guaranteed, since prices change on
  Amazon's own site independently of yours.[^amazon-associates-operating-agreement]
- **Rating and review count**: only if it is genuinely current — a
  fabricated or long-stale rating is worse than showing none at all.
- **A one-line verdict**: who the product is actually for, in one
  sentence.
- **2–3 key-feature bullets**: the specific details a reader needs to tell
  this listing apart from the next one in the same roundup.
- **A naturally-phrased call to action**: "Check Price on Amazon" or "View
  on Amazon," never a bare "click here."

## Listing Formats

- **Table format**: several products compared side by side on the same
  attributes — best when the reader is actively choosing between close
  alternatives.
- **Card grid**: a visual grid of individual listing cards, the common
  shape for a "best of" roundup.
- **Inline numbered list**: each listing as a block inside a longer-form
  article, best when the surrounding prose needs to say something distinct
  about each pick before moving to the next.

## Ordering and Ranking

If a page claims to show the "best" products, the order it lists them in
is itself a claim — say what it's ranked by (value, popularity, a specific
feature) rather than leaving a reader to assume the order is arbitrary or,
worse, paid placement.

## Disclosure on Listings

The same disclosure rule that applies to a full review applies to a
listing block: a page with several listings needs the affiliate
relationship disclosed clearly and conspicuously, not buried once at the
very top of a long page a reader may have scrolled past
already.[^ftc-endorsement-guides]

## What Kills a Listing's Conversion

| Converts | Kills conversion |
| --- | --- |
| Current price, checked recently | Stale price or a dead link |
| The product's real image | A missing or generic placeholder image |
| A specific one-line verdict | A vague "great product!" caption |
| Clear disclosure near the listing | No disclosure anywhere on the page |
| 2–3 concrete feature bullets | No detail beyond the title and price |
