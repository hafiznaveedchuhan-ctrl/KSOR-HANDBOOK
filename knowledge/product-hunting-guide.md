Choosing the right products to promote is the single biggest lever in affiliate income. A great product in the wrong niche earns almost nothing; a mediocre piece of content around a high-demand, high-commission product can earn steadily for years. This guide walks through where to look, what to look for, and how to evaluate what you find.

## Where to Look: Free Tools

### Amazon Best Sellers

Amazon's Best Sellers page (`amazon.com/bestsellers`) ranks products by sales volume, updated hourly. Every major category has its own list. This tells you what people are actively buying right now — not what you think they want.

How to use it:
- Browse the top 100 in your target category.
- Look for products ranked between #20 and #100. The top 10 are dominated by household brand names; the mid-range is where smaller creators can rank in search results.
- Check "Movers and Shakers" — products that jumped the most in rank over the past 24 hours — for emerging trends before they peak.

### Amazon's "Customers Also Bought" Section

Every product page shows related products. This is a reliable signal of what the same buyer purchases together. Following these chains uncovers product clusters and niche sub-categories that Best Sellers lists don't surface.

### Google Trends

Google Trends (`trends.google.com`) shows search interest over time. Use it to:
- Confirm a product category has stable or growing demand (not a fad that peaked two years ago).
- Compare two niches side by side before committing.
- Identify seasonal patterns — a product with a November spike is fine if your content is live before October.

A flat or slowly rising line over five years is the ideal shape for an evergreen affiliate site.

### Keyword Research Tools

Free tiers of Ubersuggest, Ahrefs Webmaster Tools, or Google Keyword Planner reveal:
- Monthly search volume for product-related queries.
- Keyword difficulty — how hard it is to rank for a given term.
- Related questions people actually ask.

Target keywords with monthly search volume above 1,000 and keyword difficulty below 30 if your site is new. These numbers shift as your domain authority grows.

## Evaluation Criteria

Not every popular product makes a good affiliate target. Run each candidate through these four filters.

### 1. Commission Rate

Check where the product's category falls in the Amazon commission table. Categories like Luxury Beauty (10%) and Handmade (5%) compound quickly; Electronics and Video Games pay 2% or less.

A product priced at $200 in a 2% category earns you $4. The same price in a 4.5% category earns $9. Over thousands of clicks, this gap is significant.

**Rule of thumb**: prioritize categories paying 4% or higher unless the product price is high enough to compensate.

### 2. Price Point

Commission percentage × product price = your gross earnings per sale. Low-priced products in low-commission categories need very high volume to produce meaningful income.

Target price ranges that tend to perform well:
- **$30–$100**: Impulse-buy range, low buyer hesitation, good conversion rate.
- **$100–$300**: Research-driven purchases; buyers read reviews carefully, which rewards thorough content.
- **$300+**: High-ticket items pay well per sale but convert less often; best suited for an audience already in research mode.

### 3. Demand Signals

Demand is not the same as popularity. Signals of genuine buying demand:
- Product has 100+ reviews and a rating above 4.0.
- Multiple competing products in the sub-category all have substantial review counts.
- Keyword research shows "best [product]" and "[product] review" queries, not just informational queries.

### 4. Competition Level

Competition among affiliate sites, not Amazon sellers. Search for the main buyer-intent keyword and look at the top 10 results:
- If the first page is dominated by major editorial sites (Wirecutter, Consumer Reports, Forbes), it will be hard to displace them.
- If you see smaller blogs with thin content, there is a gap your thorough guide can fill.
- The presence of Amazon's own category pages in the top results is a neutral signal — Amazon ranks for almost everything, but Google sends organic traffic to blogs alongside it.

## Putting It Together: A Simple Scoring Pass

For each candidate product, note:

| Factor | Question | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Commission rate | What category? | ≥4% |
| Price | What does it sell for? | $30–$300 |
| Review count | Social proof established? | ≥100 reviews |
| Keyword volume | Are people searching? | ≥1,000/month |
| Keyword difficulty | Can a new site rank? | ≤30 |
| Competition | Is the SERP winnable? | No dominant authority sites |

A product that passes all six filters is a strong candidate. One that fails one or two is still worth considering, depending on which factor it fails on — a product with low volume but no competition in a high-commission category can still be worth a single article.

## Red Flags to Avoid

- **Seasonal-only demand** with no evergreen search volume: your content earns for two months and sits idle the rest of the year.
- **Products with recurring price drops**: if a product's average price is falling, so is your commission on each sale.
- **Categories that are frequently out of stock**: Amazon's in-stock rate affects your conversion directly.
- **Gated categories**: some product types (like alcohol or firearms accessories) have additional legal and Amazon policy restrictions on how they can be promoted.
